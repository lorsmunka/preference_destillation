Study 1 · The baseline: how different are two seeds of the same student?
Question: before asking what KL, CE or size change, how much do two trainings with identical settings differ? Only a
difference larger than that is a real effect of a setting.
Size m, 10 passes, full math data. A seed fixes both the start weights and the order of the data, and the same seed is used for every loss,
so KL s1 and CE s1 start from the same weights and see the same batches: they differ only in what they learn from.

    heads' attention. Bright blocks off the diagonal are pairs sharing a seed.
  FIG 1B
Three kinds of pairs
    Each dot is a pair of students; the tick is the mean. Left: correlation; right: overlap (share of attention on the same
    tokens). The dashed line: an untrained student (random weights) against the trained ones.
  FIG 1C
Do the matched heads keep their place?
    Of the matched head pairs: the share that is the very same head (same layer and index), and the share in the same layer.
    Dashed: chance (1 in 24 heads, 1 in 6 layers).
  FIG 1D
Head roles: the same seed gives the same map
    Every cell is one head (rows: layers 1-6, columns: heads 1-4, in their own index). Two losses with seed 1, two with seed 2. Colour from low to high (the blue ramp; hover for values): previous token 0-0.3, copy 0-0.2, numbers 0-0.8, message 0-0.45, distance 0-120 tokens.

Study 6 · The coding agent: do they look at the lines they name?
Question: when the model writes its scope ("computer.py 45-50") after reading the whole project (~6,000 tokens), how much of
its attention is on those lines?
Students: the pilot size sweep (KD, small pilot data), CE at s, and KL m on the full data after 8 of 10 passes. 24 test scope replies,
6 per test project. Lift = the target lines' share of attention over what an even spread over the tokens would give them (1 = no focus).

    strongest student head.
Study 7 · Sentiment (part 1): ten seeds, five schedules, four data sizes
Question: with the data order fixed, so that seeds differ only in their start weights, is it still the seed that decides
attention? And does more data, or the KL / CE schedule, change it?
Part 1's students (48 wide, 3 layers, 1 head each; teacher Gemma 3 4B, bf16, its March 2026 prompt rebuilt exactly). They learned only the
class prior (accuracy at the most common answer, code review X-C1). E-01: five schedules × 10 seeds on 2,048 comments, every seed reading
the comments in the same order. Idea D: 256 to 2,048 comments, 5 seeds. 48 test comments; Gemma's instructions (before and after the comment)
are outside the student's view.
