Distilling a big language model's knowledge of one domain into a tiny model trained from scratch

A framework: a large teacher (Gemma) writes or scores the training data and stores its token distributions; a small, randomly initialised transformer with a reduced vocabulary learns from them. Shown on several domains, and on an agent: a maths tutor made of a distilled student plus a harness.

done

Part 1: the method across domains

Reddit comment labels, templated maths, post generation (teacher Gemma 3 4B). How the KL/CE mix behaves depends on how uncertain the teacher is.

in progress

Part 2: distilling an agent

One domain, the maths tutor (teacher Gemma 4 12B). Data, harness and pupil are built, and pilots ran with Gemma as the tutor. Training the student comes next.

The official brief

“Keretrendszer nagy nyelvi modellek tudásának desztillációjára kis méretű, szakterület-specifikus modellekbe és ágensekbe”. Its example is a maths tutor agent that runs on a phone and solves and explains word problems.

Novelty check (2026-10-07): no prior work was found that combines all four: a randomly initialised student, about 1M–100M parameters, one narrow domain, and a text-generating agent in a harness, all learned from an LLM teacher's token distributions. Each part has precedent on its own (TinyStories, TinyGSM, LLM4Teach, Structured Agent Distillation). The check found no evidence against the claim, which is not the same as proof.

1 · The two models

A big teacher writes the data once, and a tiny student learns from it

teacher

Gemma

  • Part 1: Gemma 3 4B. Part 2: Gemma 4 12B (48 layers, hidden size 3,840).
  • Vocabulary: 262,144 tokens.
  • Pilots run 4-bit NF4 on the 12 GB RTX 4070. Bulk data generation in bf16 on a rented RTX 5090 is planned.
  • Runs offline. It writes or scores every training example, and its logits are stored at every token.
student

A transformer from scratch

  • Random initialisation, RMSNorm, rotary positions.
  • Defaults: hidden 48, 3 layers, 1 head. At the reddit default that is 3,416,397 parameters, 96.0% of them the input embedding.
  • It reads only the task text and predicts over a reduced output vocabulary.
  • Student size for the tutor: open.

Output vocabulary: Gemma's 262,144 tokens cut down to the tokens the task needs (linear scale)

Gemma (full)262,144 post-gen (part 1)26,659 maths tutor18,357 + shared math v1 (part 1)528 reddit (part 1)525

Why cut it

  • The output head costs V × hidden + V parameters. At 262,144 tokens it would dwarf a body that is 48 wide.
  • The teacher's logits are stored, and learned, only over these tokens.
  • Four sections (vocabulary.py): the task's own answer tokens, whitespace, prompt tokens, and auxiliary common words. The auxiliary words widen the soft label. Reddit has 27 required tokens and 498 auxiliary.
  • During generation, an example is dropped if its response uses a token outside the vocabulary.
  • Tutor: every token of the problems, follow-ups, gold targets and format texts gives 18,357 tokens (output_tokens.json).

The input side too

  • The student only embeds tokens that occur in its domain: reddit 68,328, math v1 1,282 (against 262,144).
  • Open problem: a token the student has never seen raises an error. On the final tutor corpus (11,286 input tokens), 4.1% of SVAMP's tokens are unseen, and 72.5% of SVAMP problems contain at least one.
  • Fix planned: an UNK token plus about 2% input-token dropout in training.

2 · What distillation means here

At every position, the student learns the teacher's whole distribution, not just its pick

A real stored step (post-gen, Gemma 3 4B, batch_1, example 7, token 65). Gemma is writing a post:

“… I just feel like it's a basic courtesy and he's completely oblivious to my feelings▮”

What comes next? Gemma's choice was ,. It also kept a probability for each of the other 26,658 tokens in the vocabulary.

teachergold one-hottoy student

▁ = a leading space. ▁< is the start of <end>, the post-gen stop token. Hover a bar for its value.

1 · The teacher's distribution

p(“,”) = 0.478, p(“▁about”) = 0.199, p(“.”) = 0.199 … Gemma considers several continuations good. exp(entropy) = 4.95: roughly five options here.

2 · Cross-entropy sees one token

CE = −log pstudent(gold). The target is the token Gemma actually wrote, as a one-hot vector. Nothing about “▁about” or “.” gets through.

3 · KL sees all of it

KL(teacher ‖ student) = Σ pt · log(pt / ps). This is forward KL, the default divergence. The student is pushed toward the whole shape, including the alternatives (“dark knowledge”).

Toy students, computed live over these 9 buckets. The real loss sums over all 26,659 tokens.

4 · The mix

loss per token = r · KL + (1 − r) · CE
(both × T², summed over the answer's tokens)

r is the KL ratio. It anneals over training (slide 12). losses.py also has reverse KL, JS and TVD.

5 · Temperature softens the teacher

6 · Why it depends on the domain

Positions where the teacher is effectively one-hot (entropy < 0.1 nats): reddit 96.4%, math 99.7%, post-gen 20.0%. A one-hot teacher makes KL equal to CE. For example, a stored math v1 step puts p(“:”) = 0.999997.

Real logits: batches/post_generation/google_gemma-3-4b-it/batch_1.npz (softmax over the stored 26,659-token row; T = 0.5 and 2 recomputed from the same row). Loss: library/training/losses.py. One-hot shares: experiments/results/domain_entropy.csv (256 examples each).

3 · The framework

One pipeline for every domain

Domainprompt, stop, metric, vocab slice, corpus
Teacher datagenerate or teacher-forced score
Batchestokens + logits, .npz
TrainingTrainingRun dials
Evaluationmetrics logged during eval
Analysisreads logs only

Domain

One package per domain holds the teacher prompt, stop strings, task metric, its slice of the output vocabulary, and corpus.py. It is passed as an object: TrainingRun(domain=…). Built so far: reddit, math v1, post-gen, long-context recall (Virág), and the maths tutor. The tutor has one agentic variant per annotation and teacher mode, e.g. math_tutor_facts_forced.

Teacher data: two modes

generate: Gemma writes the answer greedily, and its logits over the reduced vocabulary are kept at every step. The example is dropped if it uses an out-of-vocabulary token or runs too long.
forced (teacher-forced scoring): Gemma reads a given gold target in one forward pass, and its distribution is kept at every target position. The targets stay correct and still carry soft labels. Answers are flagged with answer_correct, which is never used to drop an example.

Batches

batches/<domain>/<teacher>/batch_N.npz holds 32 examples per file: logits as bf16 bit patterns (bit-exact with the teacher), strings as JSON. Part 1's conversion went from 161.6 GB of jsonl to 34.8 GB of npz.

Training

TrainingRun dials: kl_ratio_start/_end, divergence (forward_kl | reverse_kl | js | tvd), distillation and teacher temperature, entropy-weighted KL, KL-ratio cycling, attention noise, size (hidden_dim, num_layers, num_heads), learning rate, epochs, and data size. A run is a Python object in a RUNS list.

Evaluation

Runs every epoch, plus a mini-eval every 1,000 batches. A teacher-forced pass (gold context) gives loss, token accuracy, top-k, overlap, mean target rank, both entropies and perplexity. A free-running rollout (the student's own tokens) gives student accuracy, task accuracy, validity, natural termination and length ratio. Everything goes to JSONL logs.

Analysis

from library import Analysis reads the logs and never reloads the model. Experiment groups seed replicates (mean ± std). Each experiments/*.py holds its runs and their analysis, and writes results to experiments/results/*.csv.

5 · Part 2: the agent

Agent = model + harness

The model writes one block per request. The harness does everything else.

modelthe distilled student (Gemma during the pilots)
  • reads a tagged conversation that ends in <message>
  • writes one block: a facts-first <solution> or a short <explanation>, or only the tag <out_of_scope>
  • the opening tag is the route, decided at its second token (< then solution, explanation or out)
  • never sees the instructions: the teacher prompt is Gemma's alone
harnesscode: harness.py, conversation.py, checks.py
  • holds the conversation state: the main line, the side thread, and the full transcript
  • applies the context rules that decide what each request contains
  • reads the route from the first tag, and treats an unclear route as a task (the safe side)
  • answers off-topic messages itself, with a fixed sentence
  • checks every block and asks once more if a check fails
  • tidies up: fills in a missing “= result” before a solution enters the history

The model plug

The model can be anything with generate(requests) → texts. Today that is GemmaTutor (the teacher playing the tutor). The student plugs into the same place. Each session is a small state machine with one pending request, so run_sessions batches many conversations into each model call (at most 8 on the 12 GB card).

What “agentic” means in the thesis

The model chooses a response type for each message and produces structured output that the harness checks and acts on. The novelty check suggests defining this explicitly, so a reviewer does not reduce it to “structured intent routing”.

6 · The items

Word problems whose answers come from running code

A real item (items.jsonl, sd-p1-h02-0007, slice S01, grade 2, seeded from an EngageNY problem)

problem: Ines emptied her backpack into the recycling bin, adding 9 bottles to the 54 already there. What is the new total?
def solve():
    there = 54
    added = 9
    total = there + added
    return {'total': total, 'answer': total}
follow-up: The bottles are packed into bags of 7. How many bags are filled?
def solve(state):
    bags = state['total'] // 7
    return {**state, 'bags': bags, 'answer': bags}
follow-up: The recycling center pays 4 dollars for each full bag. How much money is that?
def solve(state):
    money = state['bags'] * 4
    return {**state, 'money': money, 'answer': money}

answers [63, 9, 36] come from executing the code · shape add|div|mul · split test

The validator drops an item if any check fails

  • Every function runs in a sandbox: no imports, no I/O, a 1 s limit, through the project's own interpreter (solve() is never exec'd).
  • Every value is a whole number from 0 to 999. Division must be exact, except in S07, the remainder slice.
  • Every number in the code appears in the text, as digits or as a word (two, dozen, half…).
  • Unused digit numbers must match the distractor label.
  • Each follow-up must read the previous state.

Splits

  • train 32,377 · test 1,210 (10%, stratified by slice) · held-out shapes 415 (add>mul, sub>div, sub>add, never trained)
  • Test items and held-out shapes are cut by family before rewording and renumbering, so their copies stay out of training.
  • SVAMP: 746 human-written problems, the real-world test, single turn. Never trained on and never shown to a writer. 577 one-step, 169 two-step.

Why the code alone cannot write the labels

45% of numbers are written inline in solve(), so they have no name to read. Rewordings reuse the original's code, so its variable names describe a different story. A real verified sample label:

S07-mem-b02-0070~w1 (a rewording): “The craft shop gets 6 boxes of ribbons, with 15 ribbons in each box. The owner sells them in packs of 9 ribbons. How many packs can she make?”

Gemma is given the conversation and the exact calculations (6 * 15 = 90, 90 / 9 = 10). Facts may also come from implied words (“a pair” = 2), and a long step may be split into smaller ones.

8 · The conversation format

Typed blocks, a main line, and a side thread that a new task drops

What the model reads (pilot 2, item sd-p1-h02-0007; exact harness text)

<task>Ines emptied her backpack into the recycling bin, adding 9 bottles to the 54 already there. What is the new total?</task>
<solution> bottles already there = 54 bottles Ines added = 9 new total = 54 + 9 = 63 Answer: 63 </solution>
<task>The bottles are packed into bags of 7. How many bags are filled?</task>
<solution> new total = 63 bottles per bag = 7 number of bags = 63 / 7 = 9 Answer: 9 </solution>
<question>Wait, could you show me how you divided the bottles into bags again, but using smaller numbers so I can see how it works?</question>
<explanation>To see how it works, we can imagine having 14 bottles and putting them into bags of 2. Since 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 14, we would have 7 bags.</explanation>
<question>Why did you count out seven bags of 2 instead of just counting by twos?</question>
<explanation>Counting by twos is actually the same thing as counting the bags! When you say "2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14", you are counting each bag as you go.</explanation>
<message>The recycling center pays 4 dollars for each full bag. How much money is that?</message>
<task>The recycling center pays 4 dollars for each full bag. How much money is that?</task>
<solution> number of bags = 9 payment per bag = 4 total money = 9 * 4 = 36 Answer: 36 </solution>

The blocks

<task>+<solution> main line · <question>+<explanation> side thread · <message> the newest input, untyped · <out_of_scope> a reply tag only

Past turns carry their type. The newest input is left untyped because deciding its type is the model's first decision. Three routes: solution (a new task, or a what-if, which is a new calculation), explanation (a question about the working), and out_of_scope.

1A follow-up question opens a side thread. The explanation goes there, and the main line is unchanged.
2A second question: the thread grows.
3A new task arrives as <message>. The route request still shows the open thread, and the model opens with <solution>.
4The solve request leaves the thread out, so the model solves from a clean history.
5The message is stored as a <task> with its checked solution, and the thread is closed. The harness still keeps the questions and explanations in its full transcript.

Sources: thesis_work/harness_explainer.html (“A real conversation, step by step”); library/domain/math_tutor/conversation.py

9 · One agent turn

Route first, then act, check, and if needed ask once more

pupilsends a message (a template message during the pilots, slide 10)
↓
harnessRoute request: main line + open thread + <message>, at most 24 tokens, greedy. modelits opening tag is the branch. The student needs only two tokens for this: < and the tag's first word.
If no tag comes back (in pilot 3, 2 of 31 replies opened with bullets), the harness asks again with the reply already started as <. Starting every route with < was tried and dropped: it garbled 35 of 642 off-topic tags. If the route is still unclear, it is treated as a task (the safe side).
↓
<solution> a task or a what-if
  • harness solve request: main line only + message, starting <solution>\n, stopping at </solution>, 120 tokens, greedy
  • model facts, calculations, Answer: N
  • harness check_lines: every line is a fact or a true calculation, and the block ends in Answer: N. On failure it asks once more at T 0.7.
  • harness fills in a missing “= result”, stores the solution in the main line, and closes the side thread
<explanation> a question about the working
  • harness explain request: the same context as the route (thread kept), stopping at </explanation>
  • model at most two short sentences a child understands
  • harness check_explanation: not empty, never takes the solution back (“I missed”, “should have”, “sorry”…), and does not give in to pushback (“you're right”). On failure it asks once more at T 0.7.
  • harness stores the explanation in the side thread
<out_of_scope> not about this maths
  • harness replies itself: “This question is outside the scope of this task.”
  • The model writes nothing more.
  • The message never enters the model's context, and an open thread stays as it was.

10 · The template pupil

Follow-up messages come from templates filled from the solution on screen. Gemma only replies.

1 · The shown solution (Ines, turn 2) becomes foci, each with slots and features

new total = 63 bottles per bag = 7 number of bags = 63 / 7 = 9 Answer: 9

focus step0: a = 63, b = 7, operation_phrase = “divide 63 by 7”, other_phrase = “multiply 63 by 7” · features: divide, only_step, uses_earlier_answer, follow_up_task… The other foci here are 2 facts, the answer, 2 computed mistakes, and none.

2 · A kind is drawn by weight, then a (template, focus) pair. Each required feature doubles a template's weight, so niche templates win whenever their condition holds.

why · seed.jsonl
“Why did you {operation_phrase}divide 63 by 7?”

where_from · requires uses_earlier_answer (weight 2)
“in {calculation}63 / 7, is one number from the last question?”

3 · Pushback with a computed wrong answer (turn 1: the wrong operation on 54 + 9)

“if you {wrong_phrase}take 9 away from 54 you get {wrong_answer}45, so that's the answer”

Mistake foci: the wrong operation, using the distractor, skipping the last step, or a slip of ±1 or ±10. The tutor must hold firm.

4 · What-if: change a number and run the code again (turn 3, the last task)

“Suppose the {old}4 was {new}11. What would the answer be then?” → route <solution>

11 is new to the whole conversation. Re-running solve() gives 99, and the facts-first label is carried over and re-checked: payment per bag = 11 · total money = 9 * 11 = 99.

5 · Off-topic

“are you a robot” → <out_of_scope> → the harness: “This question is outside the scope of this task.”

The bank: 3,461 templates from 10 writer agents, 1,842 of them niche

explanationsolutionout_of_scope

43 ambiguous templates were later excluded, each with its reason (questions/excluded.jsonl). Each message after the first is about something linked to the one before: the same step, a number it used, or the step that used its result.

11 · Training records

Every training record is a context the harness would build

The corpus builder and the harness share one Conversation class, so the student learns on the same contexts it will see at run time.

solve

main line + message: a task

→ the gold facts-first <solution> block, one record per turn

explain

main line + thread + message: a question

→ Gemma's own checked <explanation> (its greedy text from the thread pass)

route

main line + open thread + message: the next task

→ only the tag <solution>: it teaches the decision exactly where it is hardest

off-topic route

main line + thread + message: off-topic

→ only <out_of_scope>, and the message never enters a later context

what-if solve

main line + message: what if…

→ the carried-over solution with the new number, checked against the re-run code. It ends the thread.

when a thread stops

If the tutor did not explain, or took its solution back, the thread ends there and no record builds on it. The solution itself is learned only from the clean-history solve record.

12 · How training works

An annealed mix of KL and CE, one example at a time

KL ratio r0 … 1
step 0960
temperature T0 … 1.1
step 0960
learning rate0 … 0.0015
step 0960

The curves are the formulas from schedules.py, ported line by line. r and T follow a cosine from start at step 0 to end at the last step. The learning rate warms up linearly over 10% of the steps to 0.0015, then follows a cosine down to 1e-5. Every E-01 run kept T = 1. The teacher's temperature follows the student's unless teacher_temperature_start is set.

Per-token loss on a real sequence, weighted by r at the chosen step

Post-gen test example (batch_97). Input: “Harden won't be healed from an injury to both eyes for at least a week I'd imagine.” Gemma 3 4B continued: “Seriously, the reports are saying it's worse than initially thought. …”. KL and CE are measured on stored E-01 checkpoints (seed 1, CPU, T = 1). Bar = r · KL + (1 − r) · CE.

The loop (trainer.py): each example is encoded as the context plus the target tokens shifted by one. One forward pass gives the student's logits at every target position, and the loss is summed over those positions. Then zero_grad / backward / step runs per example, so the effective batch is 1 (findings). AdamW is the optimiser. The schedules advance once per batch file. Evaluation after each epoch uses the same encoder.

A code detail flagged by the running code review (M1) and visible in trainer.py: the optimiser starts at the full learning rate, and the schedule is applied only after each batch. So the first batch trains at 0.0015, not at the warm-up's 0.

14 · Evaluation plan planned

Score each turn on gold history, and whole conversations on the model's own

Per turn, gold history

The test records' contexts hold the gold earlier turns, as the corpus builds them. This measures one turn in isolation: its route, its solution, its explanation. It mirrors teacher-forced accuracy (ground truth as context, optimistic).

the gap between the two = error compounding

Whole conversation, own history

The harness runs the student, and earlier turns hold its own answers, uncorrected, as in the Gemma pilots. This measures what a pupil would actually see. It mirrors student (autoregressive) accuracy.

What is scored

  • Solutions: task accuracy (the Answer equals the executed answer) per turn, slice and shape; line validity (check_lines); held-out shapes (415 items, 3,648 turns); SVAMP (746, single turn, reported per shape; needs the UNK fix first).
  • Routing: the test records; 360 free-written messages (Gemma routed 175 of 180 as intended); about 100 follow-up messages that you write by hand on test problems, planned.
  • Explanations: check_explanation pass rate (no taking the solution back, holding firm on pushback) and length; what-if answers against the re-run code.

Logged during eval, never recomputed

  • teacher-forced and student token accuracy, task accuracy, structural validity (a <solution> with an Answer, an <explanation>, or exactly <out_of_scope>)
  • top-k accuracy (k = 20), teacher–student overlap, mean target rank
  • teacher and student entropy, perplexity
  • natural termination rate and length ratio (rollouts are capped at 2× the teacher's length)

Post-run analysis only reads these logs (Analysis).

Why both evaluations are reported

  • Gold history scores the Sofia turn against the true context (ukulele = 48), so the turn's own skill is measured cleanly. It cannot see the cascade.
  • Own history shows what the pupil got: one routing slip turned into a wrong final answer two turns later, and every check passed along the way.
  • The one error was a route, not arithmetic. That is why routing is scored on its own.
