TL;DR

  • State of the field. Knowledge distillation (KD) for language models moved from word- and sequence-level KD Kim & Rush 2016 to choosing the divergence and fixing the train/inference mismatch with on-policy KD Gu et al. 2024 Agarwal et al. 2024 Ko et al. 2024. 2025-2026 work explains why mixing hard and soft labels works Wang et al. 2026, how stored top-k logits bias the student Anshumann et al. 2025, and when KD beats plain training at all Busbridge et al. 2025. Nearly every student is a pretrained model of 0.1-7B parameters.
  • From scratch is established, but big and general. Gemma 2/3 pre-train their small models with KD Gemma Team 2024 Gemma Team 2025; in a Gemma 2 ablation a 2B model distilled from a 7B teacher averages 67.7 against 60.3 from scratch. A design study maps truncation, KD/LM mixing and teacher size Peng et al. 2025. Tiny from-scratch models on teacher-written narrow text exist Eldan & Li 2023 Liu et al. 2023, but they learn from the text only (cross-entropy), not from teacher distributions.
  • Agent distillation is hot and uses pretrained students. Trajectory SFT Kang et al. 2025, span-aware KL Liu et al. 2026, top-k KD plus RL Ni et al. 2026, on-device function calling Erdogan et al. 2024 Chen & Li 2024 and harness distillation Ye et al. 2026 fine-tune pretrained models (0.5-7B where stated); span-aware KL uses GPT-2-to-LLaMA-sized students (120M-7B) whose initialisation is not stated. The closest from-scratch agentic analogue distils into a small RL policy, not a text model Zhou et al. 2024 (survey: Luo et al. 2026).
  • Where this thesis sits. A research framework that combines a randomly initialised 1-100M student, a task-derived output vocabulary with stored teacher logits over it, annealed KD/CE and temperature schedules, distribution analytics logged at evaluation time, and a model-plus-harness agent trained on code-verified data. Every ingredient has precedent; the combination, and the measurement of when it helps, is the contribution.
  • Defensible claims. (1) The combination above, hedged with 'to our knowledge' (see the novelty check). (2) A seed-replicated finding that the KD/CE schedule is a null on near-deterministic domains and orders the arms on a high-entropy one, which agrees with theory Menon et al. 2021 Wang et al. 2026. (3) A working phone-sized tutor whose harness checks every solution line against executed code.
  • Claims to avoid. Annealed KD-to-CE or scheduled KD share as new Clark et al. 2019 Peng et al. 2025 Zhou et al. 2024 Yang et al. 2020; entropy-aware per-token weighting as new Wang et al. 2026 Zhang et al. 2026; vocabulary reduction in general Kolesnikova et al. 2022 Goel et al. 2025 Eldan & Li 2023; stored or truncated teacher logits Gemma Team 2025 Goddard & Atkins 2024 Wang et al. 2025; 'the first token is the decision' Chen & Li 2024; 'verified' data without saying verified against what.
  • Where a reviewer will push. Teacher-forced and gold-history scores flatter (gold history lifts one model from 4.64 to 7.32 in Kwan et al. 2024; Arora et al. 2022); the 4-12B teacher is far from the capacity-gap optimum for a 10M student Zhang et al. 2025; random initialisation costs about 10 points on GSM8K in Liu et al. 2023; public test sets may be in the teacher Zhang et al. 2024.
  • Tools. TextBrewer, torchdistill, TRL's GKD trainer, DistillKit, EasyDistill and KDFlow target pretrained (mostly Hugging Face) students and scale; none builds a task vocabulary or trains a from-scratch student by default. The framework is a research instrument for that niche, not a replacement for them.

What this thesis does not do

  • No on-policy distillation (the student never trains on its own samples with teacher feedback) and no RL Agarwal et al. 2024 Gu et al. 2024.
  • No pretrained-student baseline and no smaller-teacher control so far Liu et al. 2023 Zhang et al. 2025.
  • No cross-tokenizer or cross-family teacher: the vocabulary comes from the teacher's tokenizer, so teachers from other families are not comparable (repository finding F-70a).
  • No hidden-state or attention distillation; only output distributions.
  • The harness is hand-written; nothing of it is learned or distilled Ye et al. 2026.
  • No study with real pupils and no learning-gain measurement; the pupil is a template bank plus a 360-message free-written naturalness set Macina et al. 2023 Macina et al. 2025.
  • No constrained decoding at inference; validity is learned and checked Willard & Louf 2023.
  • Single-GPU scale; no claim about throughput versus LLM toolkits Zhang et al. 2026.

Risks to the claims

The sharpest objections the literature supports, each with a mitigation.

1. 'Agent' may be read as 'structured intent routing'.

The student picks one of three tags and writes a block; the harness keeps context, checks, retries and answers off-topic messages; the pupil is a template bank. A reviewer can say the agency lives in hand-written code.

Mitigation: Define 'agent' up front with Sumers et al. 2024 and place the work in the 'knowledge in weights, harness in code' cell of Luo et al. 2026. Report route accuracy on the ~100 hand-written messages and an own-history multi-turn evaluation, not only per-turn scores.

2. The entropy claim rests on two or three points and is predicted by theory.

Teacher entropy is exp(H) of about 1.004 (maths), 1.02 (Reddit) and 2.7-2.9 (post-gen); tokenizer granularity confounds per-token entropy (repository finding F-70a), and Menon et al. 2021 and Wang et al. 2026 already predict little KD gain from a near one-hot teacher.

Mitigation: Phrase it as a measured confirmation. Add the free points the repository already lists: per-field entropy from stored Reddit logits, the teacher-temperature dial, tokenizer granularity (F-66a, F-69a).

3. Missing baselines.

No pretrained small student and no smaller teacher. Random initialisation costs about 10 GSM8K points in Liu et al. 2023, and the capacity-gap law Zhang et al. 2025 suggests the teacher is far too large. A reviewer will ask why not fine-tune a pretrained 100M model.

Mitigation: Add one pretrained-student arm and/or a Gemma 1B teacher arm, or argue the scope explicitly (phone size, vocabulary control, clean measurement of what KD alone transfers).

4. Evaluation that flatters.

Teacher-forced and gold-history numbers overstate Arora et al. 2022 Kwan et al. 2024; public benchmarks may be in the teacher Zhang et al. 2024; single accuracies hide variance across renumberings Mirzadeh et al. 2025.

Mitigation: Make autoregressive, own-history accuracy the headline; give seed and renumbering intervals; keep SVAMP strictly held out and say the student cannot be contaminated while the teacher can.

5. 'Verified' data.

Gold is consistent with an executed solve(), but nothing proves the code reads the problem correctly; TinyGSM did not check either Liu et al. 2023.

Mitigation: Report teacher-vs-code answer agreement (the answer_correct flag) as the evidence and a manual audit sample.

6. Offline-only distillation.

Exposure bias is left untreated; on-policy KD Agarwal et al. 2024 and hard/soft mixing Wang et al. 2026 target exactly the autoregressive gap the framework measures.

Mitigation: List on-policy KD as future work and connect the KD/CE results to Wang et al. 2026.

Annotated bibliography

A. Knowledge distillation: foundations (5)

Distillation trains a small student to match a teacher's output distribution instead of (or alongside) one-hot labels. The classic recipe (softened soft targets, a temperature, a mix with the hard-label loss) is still the core of almost every LLM method. Since 2020 the theory has asked why it helps (variance reduction, label information) and when it does not (optimisation limits, capacity gap).

Distilling the Knowledge in a Neural Network

Geoffrey Hinton, Oriol Vinyals, Jeff Dean · 2015 · NIPS 2014 Deep Learning Workshop (arXiv 2015)

Introduces knowledge distillation as training a small model on the teacher's temperature-softened output distribution, combined with the ordinary hard-label loss. Explains why soft targets carry 'dark knowledge' about which wrong answers are nearly right, and why the soft term must be scaled by T squared.

Relevance: The thesis loss (forward KL at temperature T, times T squared, mixed with CE) is this recipe applied per token over a reduced vocabulary.

BibTeX hinton2015distilling · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record

Knowledge Distillation: A Survey

Jianping Gou, Baosheng Yu, Stephen John Maybank et al. · 2021 · International Journal of Computer Vision 129, 1789-1819

Broad survey of distillation organised by kind of knowledge (response-, feature- and relation-based), training scheme (offline, online, self-distillation), teacher-student architecture and applications, with open challenges.

Relevance: Taxonomy for the background chapter: the thesis is offline, response-based (logit) distillation into an architecturally different student.

BibTeX gou2021survey · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

Does Knowledge Distillation Really Work?

Samuel Stanton, Pavel Izmailov, Polina Kirichenko et al. · 2021 · NeurIPS 2021

Separates fidelity (how closely the student matches the teacher) from generalisation. Students often fail to match the teacher even when they have the capacity, mainly because of optimisation difficulty, and closer matching does not always give better generalisation.

Relevance: Justifies logging fidelity (top-k agreement, KL, mean target rank) separately from task accuracy, and warns against reading 'closer to the teacher' as 'better student'.

BibTeX stanton2021does · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

A statistical perspective on distillation

Aditya Krishna Menon, Ankit Singh Rawat, Sashank J. Reddi et al. · 2021 · ICML 2021, PMLR 139:7632-7642 (arXiv title: 'Why distillation helps: a statistical perspective')

Treats the teacher as an estimate of the true (Bayes) class probabilities. A good teacher lowers the variance of the student's training objective, and a bias-variance trade-off says how much imperfect teacher probabilities can still help.

Relevance: Theory behind the entropy finding: when the teacher is almost one-hot (exp(H) near 1) its soft labels carry little beyond the hard label, so KD and CE should behave alike.

BibTeX menon2021statistical · PMLR · arXiv · checked via arXiv API + PMLR page

Towards the Law of Capacity Gap in Distilling Language Models

Chen Zhang, Qiuchi Li, Dawei Song et al. · 2025 · ACL 2025

Studies the 'curse of capacity gap': a bigger teacher does not always give a better student. Across small (under 3B) language models the best teacher size grows roughly linearly with student size; the law is used to build the MiniMA models.

Relevance: A 4-12B Gemma teacher for a 1-100M student is far outside that ratio. Acknowledge the gap and, if affordable, add a smaller-teacher control (Gemma 1B shares the tokenizer).

B. Distilling autoregressive language models (11)

For generative models the field moved from word- and sequence-level KD (2016) to a debate about the divergence (forward vs reverse KL, skewed and adaptive mixes) and about the train/inference mismatch, answered with on-policy distillation (2023-2025). The newest work (2025-2026) studies why mixing hard and soft labels works, how to store teacher logits cheaply without bias, and per-token weighting by teacher entropy. Students are almost always pretrained models of 0.1-7B parameters.

Sequence-Level Knowledge Distillation

Yoon Kim, Alexander M. Rush · 2016 · EMNLP 2016

Applies distillation to neural machine translation and introduces sequence-level KD: training the student on the teacher's beam-search outputs. Combined with word-level KD, students run 10x faster with little BLEU loss and barely need beam search.

Relevance: The 'teacher-generated' data mode is sequence-level KD; storing token logits on those sequences adds word-level KD on top, as Kim and Rush combined them.

BibTeX kim2016sequence · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

A Survey on Knowledge Distillation of Large Language Models

Xiaohan Xu, Ming Li, Chongyang Tao et al. · 2024 · arXiv preprint

Survey of distillation for LLMs organised around algorithms (eliciting knowledge, divergence- and similarity-based transfer), skills (reasoning, alignment, agents, NLP tasks) and domain verticalisation, with a strong focus on data augmentation by the teacher.

Relevance: Main overview reference for the LLM-KD section; places the thesis in white-box, divergence-based KD with teacher-generated data.

BibTeX xu2024survey · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record

MiniLLM: On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models

Yuxian Gu, Li Dong, Furu Wei et al. · 2024 · ICLR 2024

Argues that forward KL makes a small generative student spread mass over the teacher's low-probability regions, and minimises reverse KL with an on-policy, policy-gradient method. Reports better responses, lower exposure bias and calibration for 120M-13B students.

Relevance: The main argument for reverse KL. The thesis's divergence set includes reverse KL but applies it off-policy on stored teacher sequences, which is not what MiniLLM optimises; say so.

BibTeX gu2024minillm · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

On-Policy Distillation of Language Models: Learning from Self-Generated Mistakes

Rishabh Agarwal, Nino Vieillard, Yongchao Zhou et al. · 2024 · ICLR 2024

Generalized KD (GKD) trains the student on its own sampled outputs with token-level teacher feedback, mixing on-policy and fixed data and allowing any divergence (a generalised Jensen-Shannon between forward and reverse KL). Tested on summarisation, translation and GSM8K.

Relevance: The standard fix for the teacher-forced vs autoregressive gap the framework measures. The thesis does not do on-policy KD (it needs a live teacher); a clear future-work item.

BibTeX agarwal2024onpolicy · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

DistiLLM: Towards Streamlined Distillation for Large Language Models

Jongwoo Ko, Sungnyun Kim, Tianyi Chen et al. · 2024 · ICML 2024

Proposes skew KL (mixing a little of one distribution into the other for stable gradients) and an adaptive off-policy scheme that reuses student-generated outputs, up to 4.3x faster than earlier on-policy KD.

Relevance: A divergence beyond KL/JS/TVD that would be cheap to add to the framework's divergence set, and an example of scheduling how much student data is used.

BibTeX ko2024distillm · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

Rethinking Kullback-Leibler Divergence in Knowledge Distillation for Large Language Models

Taiqiang Wu, Chaofan Tao, Jiahao Wang et al. · 2025 · COLING 2025

Shows that in LLM distillation forward and reverse KL share the same optimum and both reach it given enough epochs; early on, forward KL fits the head of the distribution and reverse KL the tail. Proposes an adaptive mix (AKL).

Relevance: Supports forward KL as the default for long multi-epoch training of small students, and frames forward-vs-reverse differences as a training-budget effect.

BibTeX wu2025rethinking · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

Curriculum Temperature for Knowledge Distillation

Zheng Li, Xiang Li, Lingfeng Yang et al. · 2023 · AAAI 2023

Curriculum Temperature for KD (CTKD): a learnable temperature, trained adversarially, makes the distillation task gradually harder. A plug-in gain on CIFAR-100, ImageNet and COCO.

Relevance: Closest prior art for changing the temperature during training; the thesis uses a fixed cosine schedule rather than a learned one and should cite CTKD when describing it.

BibTeX li2023curriculum · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

BAM! Born-Again Multi-Task Networks for Natural Language Understanding

Kevin Clark, Minh-Thang Luong, Urvashi Khandelwal et al. · 2019 · ACL 2019

Distils single-task BERT teachers into one multi-task student and introduces teacher annealing: the training target mixes the teacher's prediction and the gold label, moving linearly from all-teacher to all-gold over training.

Relevance: Direct precedent for annealing the KD/CE ratio from KD-heavy to CE-heavy (0.9 to 0.1). Cite it; do not present the schedule as new.

BibTeX clark2019bam · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

Sparse Logit Sampling: Accelerating Knowledge Distillation in LLMs

Anshumann, Mohd Abbas Zaidi, Akhil Kedia et al. · 2025 · ACL 2025 (oral)

Proves that caching only the teacher's top-K probabilities gives biased estimates of the teacher distribution, hurting accuracy and calibration, and proposes random-sampling KD (importance sampling): unbiased and sparser storage, tested on 300M-3B students.

Relevance: The thesis stores the teacher distribution restricted to a fixed reduced vocabulary, a different truncation; this paper gives the terms to discuss and measure the bias of dropping mass outside it.

BibTeX anshumann2025sparse · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check

The Bridge-Garden Dilemma in LLM Distillation: Why Mixing Hard and Soft Labels Works

Guanghui Wang, Kaiwen Lv Kacuila, Zhiyong Yang et al. · 2026 · ICML 2026 (accepted, per arXiv)

Finds that mixing hard labels (teacher-sampled tokens) with soft labels (full teacher distributions) beats either alone, and that the gain comes from reduced exposure bias, not closer teacher matching. Splits steps into 'bridges' (one token is right) and 'gardens' (many are fine) and adapts the mix per step.

Relevance: The closest recent theory for the thesis's KD/CE mix and its entropy finding: near-deterministic domains are almost all bridges, so the anneal direction should matter little there.

BibTeX wang2026bridge · arXiv · checked via arXiv API record + venue cross-check
