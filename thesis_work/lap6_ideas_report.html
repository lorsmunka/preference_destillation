01

The entropy dial

STRONG
Like you're five A teacher who is always 100% sure teaches you nothing beyond the answer key — copying their confidence pattern only helps when they're sometimes unsure. Everyone believes this; nobody has ever drawn the actual curve. You can draw it three ways with data already on your disk.

The one-sentence claim The value of distillation over plain answer-copying is set by teacher entropy — shown across domains, within sequences, and causally.

The three experiments ① Across domains — already half-measured: your teachers sit at 0.004 / 0.023 / 1.06 nats, and CE ties KL on the first two but separates on the third. ② Within sequences — split the training KL into structure-token vs content-token parts from stored logits; structure tokens have ~zero teacher entropy, so all of KD's value should localize on content tokens (this is also your "perfect JSON, wrong values" gap, explained). ③ Causally — re-temper the same stored logits to dial entropy continuously on one domain, same data, same eval, and plot the KD−CE gap against entropy with 20–50-seed error bars per point.

Why it's open Menon 2021 implies it in theory but never measures it. Adaptive methods (2603.07079, 2605.01732) exploit the intuition per-token without stating the law. The closest causal predecessor is famous — Müller 2019, label-smoothed teachers distill worse — but it's confounded: label smoothing reshapes the teacher's whole logit geometry and requires retraining it. Re-tempering stored logits keeps the teacher, the data, and the ranking fixed and moves only entropy. My spot-check, not the agents': Müller must be cited as the confounded predecessor, and that's a strength, not a threat.

How it dies A reviewer says "this is the classic temperature ablation." Two honest defenses: the endpoint is anchored by construction (at τ→0 KD literally equals your CE), so the contribution is the shape of the curve — and the metric split, because your own post-gen table shows entropy may gate distributional fidelity without gating task accuracy. That refinement is the non-obvious part. If the curve turns out featureless-linear with no metric split, the result thins out.

02

Divergence zoo at 14M, offline

STRONG
Like you're five Big-model papers found that a student should imitate its teacher's favorite answers (reverse KL) rather than its whole spread (forward KL). But those students were already well-educated before distillation started. Does the advice hold for a student born knowing nothing?

Why it's open MiniLLM (2306.08543), GKD (2306.13649), DistiLLM 1/2 (2402.03898, 2503.07067): every student ≥120M and pretrained, most needing expensive on-policy sampling. With full stored logits you compute forward/reverse KL, JS, skew, TVD offline and exactly — no sampling noise, no pretraining confound. Mode-seeking assumes a capable student choosing among modes; a from-scratch 14M student may need coverage first, which could flip the accepted ranking.

How it dies Everything ties on post-gen too. Even then, "the celebrated divergence choice is a large-model phenomenon, n=30 seeds" is a citable negative. Reverse KL is gradient-unstable off-policy — use DistiLLM's skew fix and say so.

03

Multi-token prediction under distillation

STRONG
Like you're five Predicting two words ahead makes big models better and small models worse — Meta measured the crossover at ~1.3B and said so. Their explanation: the training signal for future words is too thin at small scale. Your disk holds the teacher's full probability spread for those future words. Does that rescue it?

Why it's open The crossover is documented (2404.19737: 300M and 600M get worse, gains only ≥1.3B). All MTP+distillation work since is about speeding up big pretrained models (2602.06019, KAT-V1 at 40B). Nobody has trained from-scratch <100M MTP under teacher-KL. Either sign is a result: soft targets move the crossover down, or MTP's small-scale failure is capacity, not signal sparsity.

How it dies Must run on post-gen (classification outputs are too short and deterministic), where your students are weakest — deltas could drown in noise. The seed budget is the answer, but it's a real risk. Implementation is genuinely small: one extra head, one KL term against logits shifted one position.

04

Grokking phase diagram under distillation

STRONG
Like you're five Tiny models on arithmetic famously memorize first and only "get it" much later (grokking). If the student instead copies a teacher's soft probabilities — does the long confused phase shrink, vanish, or get inherited? And does it matter whether the teacher had already "gotten it" when you copied?

Why it's open 2511.04760 (Nov 2025) showed KD from a grokked teacher accelerates grokking across distributions — but no same-distribution analysis, no temperature/α sweep, no pre-grok vs post-grok teacher ablation, no seed counts. The many-seed statistics exist only for hard labels (2607.05104, n=10 at 12K params). Distilling from a pre-grok teacher checkpoint is a clean causal test of what soft labels actually carry; label smoothing is the essential control arm (2501.04697).

How it dies Race: the 2511.04760 group is the obvious one to extend their own paper — the window is maybe 6–12 months. Also needs one new tiny algorithmic domain (modular addition) plus grokking hyperparameter archaeology before the real experiment starts.

05

Does distillation shrink the seed lottery?

STRONG
Like you're five A NeurIPS theorem says distillation reduces gradient noise. If true, fifty identical runs with different seeds should land closer together under KL than under CE. Nobody has ever checked — because nobody else can afford fifty runs per configuration. You can, before lunch.

Why it's open 2305.17581 proves partial variance reduction but validates only mean accuracy. The 3,900-model audit (2510.12615) tests means too. The measurable: σ²(KL)/σ²(CE) per metric, across α and temperature, plus trajectory statistics (time-to-valid-JSON, time-to-accuracy) — at 30–50 seeds. Zero new infrastructure.

How it dies Framing. As "we ran more seeds" it's nothing; as "testing a NeurIPS theory prediction at the outcome level, which only this rig can afford" it stands. Best used as the statistical backbone of №1–4 rather than alone.

Muon at tiny scale under KL

ADEQUATE
Like you're five The optimizer that broke the speed records and trained Kimi K2 — a 2026 paper literally calls its combination with distillation "underexplored". But "we tried an optimizer" is boring unless you pre-commit to the non-obvious questions: does it shrink seed spread, and does it interact with the rare tokens that only soft targets reward?

Why it's open No Muon study exists below 30M or under a logit-distillation loss (2601.09865 says so verbatim). Muon wins on tail/rare-class memory (2509.26030), and KD soft targets put mass exactly on rare tokens — a checkable interaction on your rare-class F1.

How it dies Reads as benchmarking; AdamW must get an equally honest LR sweep or reviewers kill it. ~50 lines of PyTorch either way.

07

nGPT as a distillation student

ADEQUATE
Like you're five A transformer whose vectors all live on a sphere can't easily shout — its output scores are capped. Your teacher is nearly always shouting (99%+ confident). Can a capped student imitate a shouting teacher at all? Nobody has ever distilled into one.

Why it's open nGPT (2410.01131) bounds logits to cosine × learned scale s_z; matching 99% mass on one of 525 tokens forces a logit gap of ~11, saturating everything else, and s_z is formally confounded with distillation temperature. Zero nGPT+distillation hits anywhere. The best pure-mechanism story of the batch.

How it dies nGPT's own headline gains are contested (the speedrun community never adopted it), the implementation is invasive (re-normalize weights every step), and the null-result risk is the highest on the board.
