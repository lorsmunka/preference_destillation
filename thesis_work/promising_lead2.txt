THE ORIGINAL QUESTION (as asked)
--------------------------------
"Double distillation - distilling from two models, needing less data from
a more expensive model. How extensively has this been studied?"

ANSWER: three readings, all three owned.
  (1) two teachers at once = multi-teacher KD. Textbook. Survey
      2006.05525; 2302.07215; 2605.27967 (Teacher-Informed Mixture
      Priors, 2026); ledger #48 already had 2204.00548 / 2402.14035 /
      2012.06048.
  (2) sequential / teacher-assistant = TAKD (Mirzadeh 1902.03393),
      IJCAI 2023, AMD 2407.04208. Known result: more TAs in the chain ->
      better student.
  (3) "less data from the expensive model" = THE ACTUAL IDEA, and the
      most thoroughly owned:
      - LLM on a Budget 2511.11574 (active KD, informative subset for the
        expensive teacher, UP TO 80% FEWER SAMPLES) - already ledger #25
      - Teaching-Assistant-in-the-Loop 2406.05322 (KD from imperfect
        teachers in LOW-BUDGET scenarios, verbatim)
      - Active LLM-based KD 2502.15685
      - NEURAL CACHING: "online distillation of a strong model into a
        weak one; when the weak model's confidence is low the strong

        weak model" = the idea mechanism-for-mechanism
      - cascade economy: MoT 2310.03094 (GPT-4 quality at ~40-47% cost),

THE SURVIVING MEASUREMENT (why this file exists)
------------------------------------------------
Flip it from a method to a measurement and something real is left:

  WHAT IS THE EXCHANGE RATE BETWEEN CHEAP-TEACHER AND EXPENSIVE-TEACHER
  LABELS, AND DOES IT DEPEND ON TASK ENTROPY?

Our law makes a specific, falsifiable, economically useful prediction:
  - if the expensive teacher's advantage is mostly BETTER-CALIBRATED
    UNCERTAINTY, then
  - on structured tasks (exp(H) ~ 1) both teachers emit the same single
    answer -> the cheap teacher should be nearly free
  - on ambiguous tasks (exp(H) ~ 3) the expensive teacher's spread over
    the plausible options is exactly where the value is -> paying pays
  => "here is when to pay for a better teacher" - the practitioner's real
  question, and the natural companion to the storage rule in lead #1.

WHY IT IS NOT SCOOPED: 2605.23857 (Strong Teacher Not Needed) varies
teacher STRENGTH (params / training tokens) on free-form LLM pretraining
and finds weak teachers work + strong teachers can saturate or reverse.
It never indexes that by TASK ENTROPY, and never touches structured
tasks. The interaction is open.

WHY IT IS NOT A HEADLINE: it grafts an entropy interaction onto an axis
2605.23857 already owns, and ledger #14 already calls teacher-swap a
knob-turn. It is a ROW beside the divergence zoo and the storage rule -
the row that makes the practical chapter concrete.


COST (the reason this is parked)
--------------------------------
Needs a SECOND TEACHER => full data regeneration for every domain in the
comparison. Reference cost from our own logs: post-generation ran ~23
s/example (123 batches, ~500 MB each, Apr 18-20). Reddit is 15,627
batches. Regeneration is days-to-weeks, not hours.
Candidate second teachers (same tokenizer family = no vocab rebuild):
Gemma 3 1B (cheaper) and/or Gemma 3 12B / 27B (dearer). Staying inside
the Gemma family keeps the reduced-vocabulary machinery untouched, which
is the difference between a week and a month of engineering.


IF IT EVER RUNS — DESIGN NOTES
------------------------------
- Hold EVERYTHING but the teacher fixed: same corpus, same student, same
  schedule, same seeds. The whole point is a clean price comparison.
- Report the SUBSTITUTION CURVE, not a winner: student quality vs
  (fraction of labels from the expensive teacher), one curve per domain.
  The interesting object is where the curves for exp(H)=1.02 and
  exp(H)=2.88 diverge.
- Mandatory control: the cheap teacher ALONE at matched total label
  count. Otherwise "mixing helps" is confounded with "more data helps".
- Cite LLM on a Budget (2511.11574) and neural caching up front as the
  method prior art; we measure the exchange rate they assume.
